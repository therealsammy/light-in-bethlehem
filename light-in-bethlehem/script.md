# Light in Bethlehem: A Christmas Musical

## Overview

A 40 to 45 minute Christmas musical for teenagers, telling the birth of Jesus with 11 songs Nigerian churches already know. The tone moves between comedy (innkeepers, shepherds, Herod) and quiet worship (the birth, the finale). Scripture references are in brackets so directors can check the story against Luke 1 to 2 and Matthew 1 to 2.

### Cast

| Character | Size of part | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Narrator 1 and Narrator 2 | Large | Confident readers. They carry the story and set up every song. One can be funny, one steady. |
| Mary | Large | Gentle but brave. Needs to hold a quiet solo moment in Silent Night. |
| Joseph | Large | Nervous, kind, slightly overwhelmed. Good comic timing helps. |
| Angel Gabriel | Medium | Strong voice, big presence. Can lead Mary's Boy Child. |
| Prophet Isaiah and Prophet Micah | Small | Dramatic, old-man voices. Fun for boys who like to overact. |
| Town Crier | Small | Gong in hand, loud, very Nigerian. |
| Madam Chioma (Innkeeper 1) | Medium | Lagos hotel manager energy. Phone always in hand. |
| Baba Tunde (Innkeeper 2) | Medium | Sleepy gateman type. Kind heart underneath. |
| Innkeeper 3 (Mallam Sani) | Small | Final innkeeper who offers the stable. |
| Shepherds (3 to 5) | Medium | Village boys and girls. Emeka is the scared one, Ngozi is the bossy one. |
| Angel choir | Chorus | Any number. White outfits, gele or simple halos. |
| King Herod | Medium | Comic villain. Big agbada, bigger ego. |
| Herod's Adviser | Small | Yes-man who agrees with everything. |
| Wise Men (3) | Medium | Agbada and fila, grand entrances, slightly lost. |
| Drummer Boy | Small | Must be able to play talking drum or djembe. |
| Children's group | Chorus | For Away in a Manger. |
| Full cast choir | Chorus | Everyone sings the big numbers. |

### Song list

1. Ekwueme (Prospa Ochimana and Osinachi)
2. O Come, O Come Emmanuel
3. Mary's Boy Child (Boney M version)
4. Chinedum (Mercy Chinwo)
5. Joy to the World (highlife arrangement)
6. Silent Night (English plus one local-language verse)
7. Away in a Manger
8. Angels We Have Heard on High
9. We Three Kings
10. Little Drummer Boy
11. Go Tell It on the Mountain, into Excess Love (Mercy Chinwo), curtain call on Imela (Nathaniel Bassey)

### Staging and props

- **Set:** one simple backdrop. Left side is Nazareth and the inns, right side is the stable and fields. Move a manger and bench in and out instead of changing scenery.
- **Props:** a gong for the Town Crier, a phone for Madam Chioma, shepherd sticks, a toy lamb, three gift boxes, a manger with a baby doll wrapped in white cloth, a star on a pole, candles or phone torches for Silent Night.
- **Costumes:** ankara and wrappers for villagers, white for angels, agbada and fila for the Wise Men, the loudest agbada in the church for Herod.
- **Band:** keyboard, talking drum, and a bass or acoustic guitar if available. Backing tracks work if you have no band.
- **Rehearsal tip:** learn the songs before blocking. Most teens already know 8 of the 11, so spend extra time on the solos in Ekwueme, Silent Night and Excess Love.

## Act 1: The Promise

*Stage is dark. A single spotlight on the two Narrators, standing far left.*

**NARRATOR 1:** Good evening, church! Merry Christmas!

*(Waits for the audience to respond.)*

**NARRATOR 1:** Ah, that one was weak. Na Christmas o! Let me try again. Merry Christmas!

*(Audience responds louder.)*

> Narrator 1: don't rush this. Wait until the church is really loud on the second "Merry Christmas". The energy of the whole show starts here.

**NARRATOR 2:** Tonight we are taking you on a journey. Not to Abuja. Not to the village. To a small town called Bethlehem, more than two thousand years ago.

**NARRATOR 1:** But before Bethlehem, there was a promise. For hundreds of years, God's people waited. They were tired. They were scattered. They wondered if God had forgotten them.

**NARRATOR 2:** But God had sent prophets with a message.

*Lights up centre. PROPHET ISAIAH enters slowly with a walking stick, very old and very dramatic.*

**ISAIAH:** *(to the audience, voice shaking)* Hear me, O Israel! A virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel! *(Isaiah 7:14)*

**NARRATOR 1:** *(whispering to audience)* He has been saying this one for a long time.

**ISAIAH:** I heard that!

*PROPHET MICAH enters from the other side, equally old, equally dramatic.*

**MICAH:** And you, Bethlehem! Though you are small among the clans of Judah, out of you shall come a ruler for my people! *(Micah 5:2)*

**ISAIAH:** *(annoyed)* Micah, this is my scene.

**MICAH:** We are both prophets, my brother. Share the stage.

**ISAIAH:** *(sighs, then to audience)* Unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given. And he shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace! *(Isaiah 9:6)*

**MICAH:** *(nodding)* Okay. That one was good.

> Isaiah and Micah: the comedy comes from taking yourselves completely seriously. Never laugh at your own jokes, even when the audience does.

**NARRATOR 2:** God made a promise. And our God is a promise keeper.

### Song 1: Ekwueme

*Full cast enters slowly from both sides while a soloist starts the song. The prophets stay on stage, hands raised. The choir joins at the chorus and the energy builds. End with everyone frozen, looking up.*

**NARRATOR 1:** *(softly, as the music fades)* But the waiting was long.

**NARRATOR 2:** Four hundred years without a word from heaven. Fathers told their sons. Mothers told their daughters. Keep waiting. He is coming.

### Song 2: O Come, O Come Emmanuel

*Lights dim to blue. A small group of 4 to 6 voices sings the first verse only, unaccompanied or with soft keyboard. The rest of the cast slowly exits, leaving one girl on stage. She is MARY.*

*Blackout.*

## Act 2: Mary and Joseph

### Scene 1: The Angel Visits Mary

*Lights up on MARY, sweeping outside her house in Nazareth and humming. ANGEL GABRIEL enters behind her in a burst of light. Mary turns, sees him, screams, and hides behind her broom.*

**GABRIEL:** Greetings, you who are highly favoured! The Lord is with you.

**MARY:** *(peeping out)* Sir, who sent you? Who are you looking for?

**GABRIEL:** Do not be afraid, Mary. You have found favour with God. You will conceive and give birth to a son, and you will call him Jesus. He will be great and will be called the Son of the Most High. *(Luke 1:30 to 32)*

**MARY:** *(lowering the broom slowly)* But how can this be? I am not even married yet. Joseph and I are only engaged.

**GABRIEL:** The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you. For nothing is impossible with God. *(Luke 1:35, 37)*

*Pause. Mary looks at the audience, then up to heaven. She puts the broom down.*

**MARY:** I am the Lord's servant. Let it be to me as you have said. *(Luke 1:38)*

> Mary: this is the turning point of the whole play. Take a full breath before you speak and say it quietly. Let the audience lean in to hear you.

**NARRATOR 2:** She was just a teenager. Like many of you sitting here. And she said yes to God.

### Song 3: Mary's Boy Child

*Gabriel leads the song. The angel choir dances in behind him with a simple side-step. Mary sits on a bench, amazed, while angels circle her. Keep it bright and joyful. Freeze at the end.*

### Scene 2: Joseph's Dream

*JOSEPH is pacing, talking to himself.*

**JOSEPH:** Pregnant? My Mary? Ah. Ah ah. What will people say? What will her father say? What will MY mother say? *(clutches his chest)* My mother will finish me.

**NARRATOR 1:** Joseph was a good man. He did not want to disgrace Mary, so he planned to break the engagement quietly.

**JOSEPH:** *(yawning)* I will decide in the morning. *(lies down on the bench and snores loudly)*

*GABRIEL tiptoes in and taps him. Joseph does not wake. Gabriel taps harder. Still snoring. Gabriel looks at the audience, then shouts.*

**GABRIEL:** JOSEPH!

> Joseph: make the snore big, and give Gabriel time for two taps. When he shouts, freeze for one second, then jump.

**JOSEPH:** *(jumping up)* Who? What? Is it armed robbers?

**GABRIEL:** Joseph, son of David, do not be afraid to take Mary home as your wife. The child in her is from the Holy Spirit. She will give birth to a son, and you are to name him Jesus, because he will save his people from their sins. *(Matthew 1:20 to 21)*

*Gabriel exits. Joseph stands still, then smiles slowly.*

**JOSEPH:** *(to audience)* Did you people hear that? Jesus! He will save his people! *(runs off calling)* Mary! Mary! I'm coming!

### Scene 3: The Town Crier

*TOWN CRIER enters, beating a gong.*

**TOWN CRIER:** Gong gong gong! Hear ye, hear ye! Listen o, everybody! By order of Caesar Augustus, the big oga in Rome, everyone must go to their home town to be counted! *(Luke 2:1 to 3)* If you are from Bethlehem, carry your load and go! If you refuse, *(beats gong)* you will hear from Caesar!

**JOSEPH:** *(entering with Mary, who is now visibly pregnant)* Bethlehem? That is my family's town. But Mary, your belle is already big. The journey is far.

**MARY:** *(taking his hand)* God told us not to be afraid. He will lead us.

### Song 4: Chinedum

*Mary and Joseph begin their journey, walking slowly across the stage. Joseph carries a bag; Mary leans on him. The choir sings from the side. Halfway through, they stop to rest and Joseph gives Mary water. They get up and keep walking. By the end of the song they reach the far side of the stage: Bethlehem.*

## Act 3: No Room

### Scene 1: The Inns of Bethlehem

*Bethlehem is busy. Extras cross the stage carrying bags and bowls on their heads, haggling and greeting each other. A sign reads "BETHLEHEM ROYAL SUITES: NO VACANCY." MADAM CHIOMA sits behind a small table, on her phone.*

**MADAM CHIOMA:** *(on phone)* I said no! Not even one room. Tell your uncle to sleep in the car. Ehen? He doesn't have car? Then he should sleep in his imagination. *(hangs up)*

*JOSEPH and MARY approach.*

**JOSEPH:** Good evening, Madam. Please, we need a room. My wife is about to give birth.

**MADAM CHIOMA:** *(not looking up)* Do you have a reservation?

> Madam Chioma: don't look at Joseph properly until "Then no room." The phone is your shield.

**JOSEPH:** A what?

**MADAM CHIOMA:** Reservation. Booking. Did you book online?

**JOSEPH:** Madam, it is the year zero. There is no online.

**MADAM CHIOMA:** *(finally looking up)* Then no room. This is census season. Everybody and their grandmother is in Bethlehem. I have six people sleeping in one bathroom. *(phone rings)* Hello? No room!

*Joseph and Mary move on. BABA TUNDE is sitting on a stool at the gate of the next inn, fast asleep, mouth open.*

**JOSEPH:** *(gently)* Baba. Baba!

**BABA TUNDE:** *(waking with a jump)* I'm not sleeping! I'm praying with my eyes closed.

**JOSEPH:** Please sir, do you have any space?

**BABA TUNDE:** *(looks at Mary, looks at Joseph, looks at the inn behind him)* My son, if I open this gate, the people inside will drag you out. They are already fighting over pillow. *(sad)* I'm sorry. No room.

**MARY:** *(holding her stomach, wincing)* Joseph...

**JOSEPH:** *(panicking, to the audience)* Church, abeg, does anybody here have a spare room?

*(Let the audience react.)*

> Joseph: really wait for the audience here. If someone shouts an answer, react to it in character before Mallam Sani comes in.

*MALLAM SANI enters, carrying hay.*

**MALLAM SANI:** I heard you. I have no room inside. But behind my house there is a stable where the animals sleep. It is not fine, but it is warm, and it is quiet.

**JOSEPH:** *(looking at Mary, who nods)* We will take it. Thank you, sir. God bless you.

**NARRATOR 1:** The King of Kings. The Lord of Lords. And the only place they could find for him was where the goats sleep.

**NARRATOR 2:** But God was not embarrassed. He chose it.

### Song 5: Joy to the World

*Bethlehem comes alive. The innkeepers, Baba Tunde and the extras lead the song in a highlife groove with the talking drum. Madam Chioma dances while still on her phone. This is a big, fun, clap-along number. On the last line, the crowd clears and the lights drop to the stable.*

### Scene 2: The Birth

*Soft blue light. The manger sits centre stage. Mary kneels beside it. Joseph stands behind her. She lifts the baby, wrapped in white cloth, and looks at him for a long moment. Silence.*

**NARRATOR 2:** *(very softly)* And while they were there, the time came for the baby to be born. She gave birth to her firstborn, a son. She wrapped him in cloths and laid him in a manger, because there was no room for them in the inn. *(Luke 2:6 to 7)*

**JOSEPH:** *(kneeling, whispering)* Jesus.

**MARY:** *(to the baby)* Emmanuel. God with us.

> Everyone: hold the silence for a slow count of five before the music starts. The quiet is part of the scene.

### Song 6: Silent Night

*Mary sings the first verse alone, or with one other voice. Cast members walk in slowly holding candles or phone torches and form a half circle around the manger. Everyone sings the second verse in a local language (Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa or Efik, whatever fits your congregation). Invite the audience to raise their phone lights too.*

### Song 7: Away in a Manger

*The children's group walks in and sits around the manger. They sing one or two verses. Keep the lights low and gentle. As they finish, they wave at the baby and exit.*

## Act 4: Shepherds and Kings

### Scene 1: The Shepherds

*Night in the fields. SHEPHERDS sit around a pretend fire. EMEKA is wrapped in a blanket, shivering. NGOZI holds a stick like a general. Someone holds a toy lamb.*

**NGOZI:** Emeka, it's your turn to watch the sheep.

**EMEKA:** Me? I watched them yesterday. And the day before. Ngozi, since January, have you ever watched these sheep?

**NGOZI:** I am the supervisor. Supervisors supervise.

**SHEPHERD 3:** *(counting the sheep, pointing offstage)* Forty-seven, forty-eight... Emeka, where is number forty-nine?

**EMEKA:** *(pulling the toy lamb out from under his blanket)* He was cold, I'm keeping him warm.

> Shepherds: keep the argument quick and overlapping, like real friends. Emeka, the lamb reveal is the punchline, so slow it down.

*Sudden bright light. ANGEL GABRIEL appears. All the shepherds scream and fall over each other. Emeka hides behind Ngozi. Ngozi hides behind Emeka.*

**GABRIEL:** Do not be afraid! I bring you good news of great joy for all the people. Today in the town of David a Saviour has been born to you. He is the Messiah, the Lord. You will find a baby wrapped in cloths and lying in a manger. *(Luke 2:10 to 12)*

**EMEKA:** *(peeping out)* A manger? Where the animals eat? The Messiah is in a manger?

**GABRIEL:** *(smiling)* That is how you will know him.

*The ANGEL CHOIR rushes in from every side.*

**ANGEL CHOIR:** Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to those on whom his favour rests! *(Luke 2:14)*

### Song 8: Angels We Have Heard on High

*The angel choir sings while the shepherds slowly stand up, still amazed. By the second chorus, the shepherds join in, even Emeka. Stretch out the "Gloria" so the choir can really belt it. At the end the shepherds grab their sticks and run toward the stable.*

**NGOZI:** *(already running)* Let's go! Emeka, bring forty-nine!

*The shepherds reach the stable, kneel quietly before the manger, then exit telling everyone they meet.*

### Scene 2: King Herod

*Lights up on KING HEROD on a throne, eating grapes. His ADVISER fans him.*

**HEROD:** Adviser, who is the greatest king in all of Judea?

**ADVISER:** You, your majesty!

**HEROD:** Who is the most handsome king in all of Judea?

**ADVISER:** *(hesitates)* ...You, your majesty!

**HEROD:** I saw that pause.

*The three WISE MEN enter grandly with their gifts. One holds a map upside down.*

**WISE MAN 1:** Greetings, O King. We have travelled from the East.

**WISE MAN 2:** Very far east. We have been on this road for many months.

**WISE MAN 3:** *(turning the map around)* Because somebody refused to ask for directions.

**WISE MAN 1:** Where is the one who has been born King of the Jews? We saw his star and have come to worship him. *(Matthew 2:2)*

*Herod chokes on a grape. The Adviser pats his back.*

**HEROD:** *(coughing)* King? Another king? In MY kingdom? *(composes himself, fake smile)* Ah, how wonderful. Adviser, where is this king supposed to be born?

**ADVISER:** *(reading a scroll)* The prophets say Bethlehem, your majesty.

**HEROD:** *(to the Wise Men, sweetly)* Go to Bethlehem. When you find the child, come back and tell me, so that I too may go and... worship him. *(Matthew 2:8)*

*Wise Men bow and exit. Herod turns to the audience and drops the smile.*

**HEROD:** Worship him? Mtchew. *(storms off, with the Adviser running after him)*

> Herod: be loud with the Adviser, sugary sweet with the Wise Men, then ice cold on "Worship him?" Three different voices.

**NARRATOR 1:** Herod had other plans. But God always has the final word.

### Song 9: We Three Kings

*A child carries the star on a pole across the stage. The Wise Men follow it, singing a verse each (or the choir sings while they act). They reach the stable, kneel, and present their gifts to Jesus: gold, frankincense and myrrh. (Matthew 2:11)*

### Scene 3: The Drummer Boy

*As the Wise Men step back, a young boy appears at the edge of the stable holding a talking drum. He looks at the expensive gifts, then at his drum.*

**DRUMMER BOY:** *(to Mary)* Ma, I don't have gold. I don't have anything expensive. But I can play my drum. Can I play for him?

*Mary smiles and nods.*

> Drummer Boy: speak to Mary, not the audience. Be small and shy with your words, then let the drum be bold.

### Song 10: Little Drummer Boy

*The choir sings softly while the Drummer Boy plays along on the talking drum. Let him take a short solo before the last verse. Mary holds up the baby toward him at the end, and the drummer boy bows.*

**NARRATOR 2:** Kings brought gold. A boy brought a drum. And God received both. Whatever you have tonight, bring it to him.

## Finale

*The whole cast gathers around the stable: Mary, Joseph, shepherds, Wise Men, angels, innkeepers and children. Even Madam Chioma is there, finally off her phone. The Narrators step to the front.*

**NARRATOR 1:** So that's the story. A promise made by God, kept by God.

**NARRATOR 2:** A teenage girl who said yes. A carpenter who chose to trust. Shepherds nobody respected, the first to hear the news. Kings who travelled for months just to kneel.

**NARRATOR 1:** And a baby in a manger, because there was no room.

**NARRATOR 2:** *(to the audience)* This Christmas, there will be rice, chicken, new clothes, and plenty of visitors. All of that is good. But the question is the same one Bethlehem had to answer.

**NARRATOR 1:** Is there room for Jesus?

> Narrator 1: this is the question of the night. Look at the audience, not the floor, and leave a real pause before Madam Chioma's line.

**MADAM CHIOMA:** *(stepping forward)* I have room now!

*(The cast laughs.)*

**NARRATOR 2:** The shepherds didn't keep the news to themselves. They went and told everybody. *(Luke 2:17)* Tonight, so will we.

### Song 11a: Go Tell It on the Mountain

*Up-tempo, full band. The cast comes down from the stage into the aisles, clapping and inviting the congregation to stand and sing. Shepherds, angels and Wise Men shake hands with the audience.*

### Song 11b: Excess Love

*The music slows. The cast returns to the stage. A strong soloist leads, with the full choir on the chorus. Mary holds up the baby. Lights narrow slowly to a single spotlight on the manger by the final chorus. Hold the silence for three seconds after the last note.*

**NARRATOR 1 and 2 together:** For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son. *(John 3:16)* Merry Christmas!

### Curtain Call: Imela

*Lights up full. The band starts Imela. Cast members bow in groups: children, angels, shepherds, innkeepers, Herod and Adviser (expect boos and laughs), Wise Men, Drummer Boy, Prophets, Narrators, and finally Mary and Joseph. The whole cast sings the chorus together as thanksgiving and invites the congregation to join.*

*End.*

### Director's notes

- **Running time:** about 20 minutes of drama plus 11 songs. Cut a verse from the longer carols if you need to land under 45 minutes.
- **Easy cuts:** O Come, O Come Emmanuel and Away in a Manger can be dropped without breaking the story.
- **Local flavour:** rename the comic characters and change the jokes to match your church and city. Inside jokes about your own pastor or choir director (kept kind) always land.
- **Audience moments:** Joseph asking the church for a room, the phone lights in Silent Night, and the cast coming into the aisles keep the congregation involved.
